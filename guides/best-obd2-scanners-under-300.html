What matters more than the product name

  • System coverage: A basic OBD2 reader usually focuses on emissions-related powertrain diagnostics. ABS, SRS/airbag, transmission and body modules require supported enhanced coverage.
  • Bidirectional control: This sends commands to supported vehicle modules to activate components or run tests. It is different from reading and clearing fault codes.
  • Vehicle compatibility: A feature listed on a scanner's box may not be available on your exact year, make, model, trim or module.
  • Phone vs. screen: Bluetooth dongles depend on an app and compatible phone. Handheld readers have their own display but may offer fewer advanced functions.
  • Ongoing costs: Check app fees, update renewals, subscriptions and any paid vehicle-specific add-ons.

The A30M V2.0 is the most feature-rich option in this shortlist on paper. XTOOL's product information lists full-system diagnostics, active tests, 26+ service/reset functions, graphing for up to eight live-data streams, CAN FD and FCA AutoAuth support. It connects wirelessly to a phone or tablet rather than providing its own large display.

Why consider it: You want to go beyond reading codes and need supported active tests such as commanding selected pumps, injectors, A/C clutches, windows or other components.

2. OBDLink MX+ — best for enhanced data and app flexibility

The MX+ is a Bluetooth adapter that works with iOS, Android and Windows. OBDLink lists support for standard OBD-II protocols plus Ford MS-CAN and GM SW-CAN networks, and includes free OEM-specific add-ons for supported vehicles. Its official product page lists a $139.95 price at the time of research, a three-year warranty and a 180-day money-back guarantee.

Why consider it: You want manufacturer-specific data, live parameters and the flexibility to use supported apps such as FORScan Lite, OBD Fusion or other compatible software. It is especially worth researching for compatible Ford and GM vehicles.

What to check: The MX+ is an adapter, not a tablet with its own screen. Enhanced module access depends on vehicle coverage and the app. Do not buy it expecting a broad menu of standalone bidirectional active tests; its strengths are data access and app compatibility.

3. BlueDriver Bluetooth Pro — best for DIY repair information

BlueDriver pairs a Bluetooth OBD2 adapter with its iOS/Android app. Its product materials describe enhanced diagnostics for supported systems such as ABS, airbag/SRS and transmission, along with repair reports that help explain possible causes and fixes. BlueDriver advertises no subscription fee for its core app experience.

Why consider it: You want a straightforward phone-based tool that does more than generic engine codes and provides repair information in the app.

What to check: Enhanced code coverage is vehicle-specific. BlueDriver is not a bidirectional scan tool, does not provide a standalone screen and should not be purchased for active component tests.

The NT301 is a wired handheld code reader with its own color display. It is designed around standard OBD-II engine/emissions diagnostics, including reading and clearing check-engine codes, viewing live data and checking emissions readiness. It is a simpler tool than the other options here.

Before you order: five-minute compatibility check

  1. Write down your vehicle's year, make, model, engine and trim.
  2. Identify the exact task: engine code, ABS code, airbag code, transmission data, oil reset, ABS bleed, active test or something else.
  3. Use the scanner maker's official coverage checker or ask support to confirm that exact task for your vehicle.
  4. Check whether the feature requires a paid app, subscription, update renewal, security gateway authorization or extra cable.
  5. Confirm the Amazon listing's model/version, seller, included accessories, return policy and current price.
Safety note: Active tests and service procedures can operate vehicle components. Follow the manufacturer's service instructions, use appropriate precautions and do not run a procedure unless you understand its purpose and risks.

Bottom line

There is no single best scanner for every driver. The XTOOL A30M V2.0 is the candidate to investigate when bidirectional control is the priority. OBDLink MX+ is a better fit when enhanced data and app flexibility matter. BlueDriver suits DIY owners who want guided diagnostic reports, while FOXWELL NT301 is enough for basic engine-code work.

Do not buy based only on the number of advertised resets or a generic compatibility claim. Verify the exact function on your exact vehicle before paying.
