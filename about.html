Our approach

Useful guidance, without the sales noise.

Scan Tool Field Guide is an independent informational project focused on automotive diagnostic equipment. Our aim is to help readers understand scan-tool capabilities, vehicle compatibility, update policies and practical trade-offs before they buy.

How we research products

Our guides are primarily desk research. Unless a page explicitly identifies a hands-on test, we have not physically tested that scanner. We do not present manufacturer claims as independent test results.

  1. Start with primary sources. We look first for manufacturer specifications, manuals, official coverage tools, update policies, warranty terms and support documentation.
  2. Separate facts from judgment. Published hardware and feature claims are described as manufacturer-stated. Our recommendations are editorial assessments based on the stated use case, not laboratory rankings.
  3. Check the whole ownership picture. Where information is available, we compare included updates, renewal requirements, subscriptions, optional adapters, warranty and regional restrictions.
  4. Identify uncertainty. If a manufacturer coverage tool does not confirm a precise procedure, we say to ask support. A broad feature count is not proof that a function works on a particular vehicle.
  5. Keep information reviewable. We include source links and update dates where available. Prices, inventory, bundles and software policies can change, so readers should confirm current terms before buying.

How we make recommendations

We match a product to the stated diagnostic task, device preference, budget and documented limitations. A recommendation is a shortlist for further verification—not a promise that the scanner supports every module or service function on your vehicle. We do not claim hands-on testing unless the page clearly says so.

What we do not claim

We do not claim to be a repair shop, a vehicle manufacturer, an authorized distributor or a certification body. Unless a page explicitly says otherwise, our product information is desk research—not independent laboratory testing.

Safety

Diagnostic tools do not replace service information, training or safe repair procedures. Work involving airbags, brakes, steering, high-voltage components or vehicle programming may require qualified professionals.

Corrections and updates

Product details and coverage can change. If you find an outdated specification, broken link or incorrect statement, use the public project issue tracker to report the page and the specific issue. We can then review it against the relevant primary source.
